# Four-day workweek: 60-person professional-services firm

**Research date:** 21 October 2026. **Determination:** Run a limited 90-day pilot with strict eligibility and stop rules; do not permanently introduce the policy until a pilot demonstrates client service and economic neutrality.

## 1. Evidence review (as of 21 October 2026)

**Comparison population.** The decision concerns a hypothetical 60-person professional-services firm; specialty, location, billing model, timekeeping, client service-level agreements, pay policies and baseline operating data were not specified. Comparable evidence means small-to-medium knowledge and client-service organizations, rather than treating results from shift-based operations as directly transferable.

- **Highest-quality recent worker-outcome evidence:** Fan, Schor, Kelly & Gu, *Nature Human Behaviour* (21 July 2025), studied 2,896 employees at 141 organizations in six countries over six months, following preparation/reorganization, and compared outcomes with 12 control companies. Lower burnout and better job satisfaction and health were observed. **This measures well-being, not objectively audited billable productivity or client service.** Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40691306/.
- **UK multi-employer organizational evidence:** UKRI's 2024 summary of the 2022 six-month UK trial covers 61 organizations; leaving rates fell 57%, sick days 65%; average reported revenue increased 1.4% among **only 23 companies providing revenue data**. At least 54 of the original 61 still followed a form of the policy at one year, according to the Autonomy follow-up. **Self-selected employers, missing revenue data, no randomized company assignment, and different countries restrict causal and firm-level extrapolation.** Sources: https://www.ukri.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-doing/research-outcomes-and-impact/esrc/making-the-case-for-a-four-day-working-week/ and https://autonomy.work/portfolio/making-it-stick/.
- **Most relevant size/service comparison:** University of Münster's 2024 project covered 45 German organizations, including consulting and services, media and IT; many were small (10–49) or medium (50–249), bracketing the target's 60 employees. Turnover and profit increases were not statistically distinguishable from the preceding year. Two employers exited early because of economic pressure or weak internal backing; the retained analysis included 41. Some varied the reduction and gave time off differently. The university's March 2026 follow-up reports favorable recruitment and retention perceptions **among organizations still using reduced hours**; this is subject to survivor/response bias. Sources: https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=14338 and https://www.wiwi.uni-muenster.de/fakultaet/de/news/5465.
- **Counterevidence/implementation risks:** CIPD's employer analysis reports, among organizations that had reduced working hours, 32% said the arrangement did not suit everyone, 30% cited inability to maintain output and 26% said tasks required physical presence. **This is a respondent survey, not a causal failure rate for professional-services firms**, but directly refutes guaranteed output preservation. In its July 2025 survey, 45% of employees said they would use a reduced-hours four-day week without pay loss if available, while 61% of employers viewed flexibility advertising as important to hiring; **stated preference is not a measured hiring lift.** Sources: https://www.cipd.org/uk/views-and-insights/thought-leadership/cipd-voice/employers-perspectives-four-day-working-week/ and https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/reports/flexible-hybrid-working/.

**Evidence judgment:** Strong directional evidence on employee well-being; moderate, selection-sensitive evidence on retention and employer appeal; inconclusive direct evidence that a 60-person fee-earning firm can maintain client service, absolute deliverable throughput, and contribution margin after reducing weekly hours. A controlled local pilot can test that uncertainty; permanent implementation is not supported by the literature alone.


## 2. Operating conditions: success and failure

| Condition | More likely to succeed | More likely to fail |
|---|---|---|
| Work design | Eliminate low-value meetings, duplicate reporting and avoidable handoffs **before** reducing hours; preserve focus time | Shorten the calendar but leave the same workload, deadlines, and meeting structure in place |
| Client coverage | Stagger days off by pod, provide named backup and meet unchanged response windows, deadlines and escalation SLAs | Entire firm closes Friday while contracts and clients expect five-day or emergency access |
| Economics | Track deliverables, realized rates, utilization, rework and contribution margin; use freed capacity instead of relying on optimism | A 20% reduction in paid productive hours forces unbudgeted hires/overtime or loses billable work |
| Personnel and fairness | Differentiate roles; provide equivalent benefit for noneligible/on-call roles; make workload boundaries credible | Unequal access causes resentment, uncovered critical roles, or managers working a fifth day regardless |
| Leadership | Give each team a workload redesign plan, scheduling tools and active issue review | Weak sponsorship, peak-cycle shocks, or inability to resolve cross-team bottlenecks |

**Observed checks on optimism:** In Münster's mixed-sector trial, two of the 45 firms exited early amid economic difficulties or inadequate internal support (https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=14338). CIPD survey respondents who had reduced hours reported output shortfalls (30%), unsuitable role patterns (32%) or presence requirements (26%); percentages describe reported difficulties, not causal four-day-week failure probabilities (https://www.cipd.org/uk/views-and-insights/thought-leadership/cipd-voice/employers-perspectives-four-day-working-week/). The 2025 controlled multi-country study specifically describes pre-trial work reorganization, not an unprepared Friday closure (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40691306/).

**Contractual assumption:** There is no evidence about this specific firm's service mix, revenue model, or peak periods. Proposed eligibility and service gates below are **decision safeguards**, not empirical findings about the firm.


## 3. Likely effect on the six required dimensions

| Dimension | Evidence-supported direction and practical inference | Confidence / key test |
|---|---|---|
| **Productivity** | Well-being and self-reported work ability tend to improve; UK trial revenue was broadly steady (1.4% for just 23 reporters) and German revenue/profit were not significantly changed. **Neither proves equal billable output per employee.** A 32-hour schedule instead of 40 would require **25% more output per working hour** to preserve unchanged total employee output: 40/32 − 1 = 25%. | **Uncertain** for this firm. Measure accepted deliverables, billable realized revenue, billable hours, rework and backlog, not hours alone. |
| **Client service** | The sources have limited standardized, independently verified client response and SLA data. Continuity may be maintained with rota coverage, but lost availability can damage time-sensitive clients; CIPD identifies presence requirements as an obstacle. | **Low confidence.** Audit contracted SLAs, on-time delivery, time-to-response, complaints and renewal risk. |
| **Recruiting** | Employees express strong preference (45% in CIPD 2025 would use a reduced-hours four-day week); Germany's 2026 continuation subgroup reports more applications (53%) and stronger employer attractiveness (74%). Those are not verified changes in this firm's applicant quality or time-to-fill. | **Moderate directional, weak causal.** Track qualified applicants/offer, offer acceptance and fill time versus prior comparable roles. |
| **Retention** | UK trial participant staff departures fell 57%; Germany's 2026 continuation subgroup reports stronger binding (56%). These are self-selected, and retention changes cannot be credited entirely to the schedule. | **Moderate but selection-sensitive.** Track voluntary attrition, regrettable loss, engagement and fatigue; 90 days may be too short to detect stable retention changes. |
| **Management burden** | Scheduling, handoffs, availability coverage, capacity allocations and fairness reviews add immediate work. Work redesign can eventually remove meetings and improve decision discipline. Trial studies had workshops and preparation; no robust quantitative management-hour estimate transfers to this firm. | **Likely higher initially; uncertain net.** Track incremental supervisor hours, escalations, schedule failures, and after-hours interruptions. |
| **Cost** | Salary remains level if adopting the no-pay-cut format, so a 20% hours reduction raises compensation cost **per scheduled hour by 25%** (40/32 − 1), before any added coverage. Economic cost can be offset by efficiency, lower attrition/absence or higher realization, but these savings have not been demonstrated for this firm. | **Material downside risk.** Test gross margin, net billable realization, overtime, contractors, coverage hires, avoided recruiting cost. |

*Source anchors:* 2025 controlled trial (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40691306/); UK business outcomes (https://www.ukri.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-doing/research-outcomes-and-impact/esrc/making-the-case-for-a-four-day-working-week/); Münster mixed-sector and follow-up (https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=14338; https://www.wiwi.uni-muenster.de/fakultaet/de/news/5465); CIPD current flexibility preference (https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/reports/flexible-hybrid-working/) and employer obstacles (https://www.cipd.org/uk/views-and-insights/thought-leadership/cipd-voice/employers-perspectives-four-day-working-week/).

**Scenario arithmetic is illustrative**, assuming a conventional 40-to-32-hour reduction, not a user-selected or company-verified arrangement. A compressed 4×10 schedule has different cost/benefit, and fewer weekly hours could instead be 36 or another value; no model is prescribed by the contract.


## 4. Assessment of introducing a four-day workweek

**Do not introduce a permanent four-day policy now.** The evidence supports likely employee benefits but does not show that this unknown firm's five-day client obligations, billable deliverables or profitability could be preserved. Six-month international trials often included extensive redesign and adaptation, while the firm's service mix, current workload, hourly economics and SLAs are unknown. This does **not** rule out a bounded pilot in teams whose output and coverage can be measured.

The strongest recent multi-country study measures changes in burnout, satisfaction and health, rather than verified billable throughput or client-response performance (2025: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40691306/). More directly analogous German participants included consulting and services firms in the small and medium size bands, but the study found no statistically significant year-over-year financial improvement and two participants withdrew (2024: https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=14338). CIPD employers identify output and role-coverage obstacles that matter for contractual professional-services delivery (https://www.cipd.org/uk/views-and-insights/thought-leadership/cipd-voice/employers-perspectives-four-day-working-week/).

Crucial configuration choices are still undecided: reduced hours versus four longer days; unchanged salary versus changed pay; staggered days versus a shared day off; and which client-hours promises must be honored. The pilot proposal will explicitly label its recommended configuration as a testing assumption—not assert that the firm has approved it. This is a **decision to gather controlled local evidence** before contemplating permanent adoption, rather than a presumption that a four-day workweek automatically succeeds.


## 5. Recommendation and evidence gate

**Run a narrowly gated 90-day pilot; do not commit to firmwide permanent adoption.** On balance, research supplies a plausible, testable benefit (better well-being and potentially lower attrition) and some comparably sized/service-sector implementation experience. The demonstrated uncertainty is precisely whether a given professional-services firm can protect client service and unit economics. The pilot is justified **only as a capped, reversible experiment** with unchanged client service, independent accounting, explicit stops and eligibility based on coverage. An unguarded firmwide rollout is not justified.

**For:** 2025 six-country comparison with control firms reported well-being improvements; UK participants saw fewer departures and broadly stable reported revenue; German comparators include consulting/services and 50–249-person companies. **Against:** the controlled study does not objectively establish billable throughput; UK revenue evidence covers only 23 organizations; Germany recorded two early exits; CIPD respondents described output and role-coverage obstacles; selection and foreign-market transfer weaken generalization. Strong enough to **test**, not strong enough to **guarantee success**. A 90-day window is shorter than the cited six-month trials, so durable retention or net financial benefits cannot be confirmed in the same period.

The approval to initiate actual operational changes remains with firm leadership; this is an analytical recommendation, not authorization or an assertion of management approval.

**Direct source record:** 2025 multi-country controlled well-being study (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40691306/); 2024 German service/SME comparison (https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=14338); 2024 UK trial revenue and departures (https://www.ukri.org/who-we-are/how-we-are-doing/research-outcomes-and-impact/esrc/making-the-case-for-a-four-day-working-week/); CIPD obstacles (https://www.cipd.org/uk/views-and-insights/thought-leadership/cipd-voice/employers-perspectives-four-day-working-week/). These results are neither a client-service guarantee nor an estimate of this firm's gain.


## 6. Conditional 90-day pilot design — evidence gate met

**Aim and treatment.** Test a *reduced-hours* four-day arrangement with **32 hours/week, 100% base salary and unchanged contracted client service**, against comparable nonpilot teams or the firm's own seasonally adjusted historical baselines. These are **proposed experiment parameters**, not contract facts or management decisions. 4×10 compressed hours would not test the same hypothesis. Stagger the off-day so the firm continues normal Monday–Friday coverage. Avoid pay cuts; maintain normal employment and overtime compliance subject to HR/legal review. Run the **90 days of measurement after readiness sign-off**; collect pre-pilot baseline data in the four preceding weeks so preparation is not silently charged to the test.

### Eligibility and fairness

- Enter only voluntarily consenting teams with (1) at least four weeks of verified baseline information on deliverables, realized billing, service SLAs, workload and costs; (2) identified primary/backup coverage for every critical client and weekday; (3) planned deadlines and seasonal peak coverage demonstrably feasible with 32 hours; (4) a manager approved to audit work intensity, enforce a no-hidden-fifth-day rule and report weekly; and (5) no uncovered regulatory, contractual or on-call requirement.
- Use **two to three functionally coherent teams (approximately 12–24 people total as a planning range, not a firm fact)**. Keep similar nonpilot teams on current schedules for matched comparison where feasible; do not presume randomization. Exclude roles whose operational coverage cannot be made safe; offer those employees other flexible choices or later eligibility review to avoid two-tier inequity. Do not exclude individuals based on protected characteristics, tenure stereotypes or personal obligations.
- Allow a team to decline and use a different off-day if a key client needs Friday availability. Put a named duty lead in place for daily escalations. Record all out-of-hours client coverage as working time; no unpaid workaround.

### Measurements and decision thresholds

The following are **proposed management guardrails, not outcomes predicted or demanded by research**. Calculate comparisons against a preceding four-week baseline **and**, where feasible, matched unpiloted teams and the same prior-year season. Pre-approve statistical/sample limitations.

| Area | Core measures | Day-90 continuation gate |
|---|---|---|
| Client service (hard) | % SLA compliance, median/95th percentile first response, on-time deliverables, material complaints | **No material SLA breach caused by coverage; ≥ baseline SLA compliance; ≥ 95% of baseline on-time delivery**, with contract-specific stricter promises overriding |
| Productivity (hard) | Accepted deliverables per paid FTE, realized net billable revenue per paid FTE, rework and backlog | **≥ 98% of baseline accepted output and realized revenue per paid FTE**, after demand/seasonality adjustments; no material growth in backlog or rework |
| Economics (hard) | Project gross margin, overtime/contractor spend, revenue leakage, added paid management time | **No more than 2 percentage points gross-margin deterioration attributable to pilot**, and no net recurring incremental coverage cost without identified offset |
| Employee effects | Burnout/fatigue pulse, absence, satisfaction, after-hours work | **≥ 10 percentage-point improvement in favorable fatigue/well-being response**, no increase in reported overwork or unrecorded hours; interpret small samples cautiously |
| Recruiting/retention | Qualified applications, offer acceptance, regrettable departures and intent-to-stay | **Report change without pass/fail claims if sample insufficient**; do not require a statistically implausible 90-day turnover win |
| Management overhead/fairness | Extra manager coordination hours, callouts, uncovered roles, eligibility complaints | **No sustained average > 2 extra coordination hours per manager per week** without an agreed offset; no unresolved disparate burden |

### Reviews and stop conditions

- **Day 0 readiness checkpoint:** Baseline approved by finance/HR/client leads; risk register, schedule and individual coverage matrix signed; every client contract checked; clear escalation owner. **Do not start an ineligible team.**
- **Days 1–14:** Weekly reviews of daily SLA exceptions, deliverables, tracked working time, overtime, manager handoff load and employee fatigue. Correct routing immediately; log deviations from the 32-hour treatment.
- **Day 30:** Formal go/adjust/stop review against client, output and cost indicators; check unintended work intensification and sample composition. Restore five-day coverage for any failed pod while preserving other eligible pods.
- **Day 60:** Repeat review with finance and client feedback; compare seasonality and pipeline shifts; check retention/recruitment only descriptively where data are sparse.
- **Day 90:** Decision report covering all six domains; compare contemporaneous control where available; decide stop, extend research, modify, or recommend broader rollout **only with separate leadership approval**. The 90 days do not establish long-term retention impacts.

**Immediate stop / suspend a team:** a serious client SLA breach attributable to unavailable coverage; an uncovered contractual/regulatory duty; repeated missed critical deadlines or an acute quality incident; material hidden or uncompensated fifth-day work; substantial safety/wellbeing deterioration; or contribution-margin deterioration of **more than 5 percentage points for two consecutive reporting weeks attributable to the trial**. Any single serious risk triggers a rapid review and temporary restoration of prior schedule. Treat these as designed safeguards, not evidence-derived universal cutoffs.

**Cost sensitivity before activation:** For 60 people, 32 hours instead of 40 removes up to 480 scheduled employee-hours/week if applied firmwide (60×8); **this pilot does not** apply to all 60. For 12–24 pilot employees it removes 96–192 scheduled hours/week, requiring demonstrable elimination of low-value work, improved effective output/hour or compensated capacity. At unchanged pay, cost per scheduled hour increases 25%; preserving the same volume requires 25% higher output per scheduled hour. Pilot accounting must distinguish scheduled hours, billable hours, priced deliverables and retained cash margin.

**Design sources and limits:** The 2025 controlled six-month trial incorporated work redesign before reduced-hours operation (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40691306/). The German SME and service-sector pilot used variations in schedule and involved managerial preparation (https://www.uni-muenster.de/news/view.php?cmdid=14338). CIPD's employer survey documents obstacles in output and job coverage (https://www.cipd.org/uk/views-and-insights/thought-leadership/cipd-voice/employers-perspectives-four-day-working-week/). These sources justify measurable safeguards, **not** any claim that the numerical 90-day thresholds are validated universal benchmarks.
